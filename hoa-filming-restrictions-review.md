# Las Brisas HOA — Filming Restrictions Review

**Source:** https://www.ciaservices.com/cia-community-documents.php?id=194432
**Scope reviewed:** 41 governing documents (Articles, Bylaws + Amendment, Master Declaration, all 10 Phase Restrictive Covenants + amendments, all Annexation Declarations, ACC Extensions, Management Certificate, Policies (Collection, Records Production, Records Retention, Security, Flag/Sign), Marina & Curbing Rules).

---

## Bottom line

**There is no explicit prohibition on filming, photography, video recording, drones, or media production anywhere in the Las Brisas governing documents.** None of the words *film, video, photograph, drone, broadcast, cinema, motion picture, production* appear in any covenant, bylaw, or rule as a restricted activity. The only direct hits for "video / photograph / camera" are administrative (records-copy fee schedule) or about home security cameras.

That said, the HOA does have **several indirect restrictions** that an enforcement-minded Board or a complaining neighbor could try to use to challenge filming at your house — especially commercial filming, large crews, equipment trucks, or anything that creates noise/light/traffic. Below is every verbatim clause that could conceivably be invoked, with citation.

---

## 1. Direct keyword scan — what does NOT exist

Across all 41 documents, **0 hits** for: `film`, `drone`, `UAV`, `broadcast`, `cinema`, `motion picture`, `media production`, `crew`, `lens`.

Hits for video / photo / camera, in context:

| Term | Document | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| `video / photograph` | Policy-Records-Production | Per-page copy fees for DVDs, VHS, photographs — administrative, **not a restriction**. |
| `camera`, `recording` | Policy-Security (Mar 2024) | Permits **security** cameras on your own property; restricts them from facing/recording neighbors. **Not a restriction on personal filming**; only on surveillance pointed at others. |

> Verbatim, Security Policy §2: *"Security cameras may be installed on an owner's property. Security cameras may face into and record any portion of the owner's property. For privacy reasons, security cameras shall not face into or record the private property of others such as a neighboring property."*

That language is about fixed surveillance cameras, but a careful reader should note the **"private property of others"** principle — the HOA has on the record that it considers pointing recording equipment at neighbors a privacy violation. A hostile neighbor could try to extend this to a film camera pointed across a property line.

## 2. Indirect restrictions that could be invoked

These appear in essentially identical form across the master *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, the *Declaration of Covenants, Conditions & Restrictions (1987)*, the *Amendment to Phase I & II Restrictive Covenants (1989)*, and every per-phase Restrictive Covenants document (Phases I–X, including all Phase VI amendments).

### 2a. Single-family residential use only (Section I — Land Use)

> *"Said Lots be known and described as single-family residential Lots. **Unless otherwise approved by the Architectural Control Committee**, such Lots are to be used for single-family residential purposes only."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §I (and identical or near-identical text in Amend-Phase1&2 §I, RC-Phase1 §I, RC-Phase2 §I, RC-Phase3 §I, RC-Phase4 §I, RC-Phase5 §I, RC-Phase6 §I and its three Amended & Restated versions, RC-Phase7 §I, RC-Phase8 §I, RC-Phase9 §I, RC-Phase10 §I.)

**Why it matters for filming:** "Residential purposes only" is the standard hook HOAs use to argue that **commercial filming** (paid shoots, productions, rentals to film companies) is a prohibited "non-residential use." Personal/family filming is almost certainly not implicated. Note the carve-out — the ACC *can* approve other uses. That means commercial filming explicitly *requires written ACC approval* under this clause.

### 2b. Nuisance / noxious / offensive activity (Section XXI or XXII)

> *"No noxious or offensive activity shall be carried on upon any Lot or common area, nor shall anything be done thereon which may be or may become an annoyance or nuisance to the neighborhood."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §XXI (matched at RC-Phase1 §XXII, RC-Phase2 §XXI, RC-Phase3 §XXI, RC-Phase4 §XXI, RC-Phase5 §XXI, RC-Phase6 §XXI and all three amendments, RC-Phase7 §XXI, RC-Phase8 §XXI, RC-Phase9 §XXI, RC-Phase10 §XXI, Amend-Phase1&2 §XXII.)

**Why it matters for filming:** This is the catch-all. A neighbor who finds your shoot disruptive — closed road, generator noise, crew foot traffic, late hours — can complain that it is an "annoyance or nuisance to the neighborhood." It is intentionally vague.

### 2c. Exterior lighting (same section)

> *"No exterior lighting of any sort shall be installed or maintained on a Lot where the light source is offensive or a nuisance to neighboring property (except reasonable landscape lighting that has the approval of the Architectural Committee)."*
> — Same section as above, all documents listed in §2b.

**Why it matters for filming:** Movie lights, HMIs, balloon lights, and even consumer LED panels can be argued to be "offensive or a nuisance" to neighbors. Approval is required even for landscape lighting; a literal reading would require approval for any production lighting.

### 2d. Exterior speakers / sound devices (same section)

> *"No exterior speakers, horns, whistles, bells or other sound devices (except security devices used exclusively to protect the lot and the improvements located thereon) shall be placed or used upon any Lot without approval of the Architectural Committee. Telephones will be allowed outdoors."*
> — Same section, all phase covenants.

**Why it matters for filming:** Outdoor PA, playback speakers, bullhorns, or boom-mic monitoring through a powered speaker would arguably need ACC approval. Lavalier mics and a single boom would not.

### 2e. Commercial vehicles (Section preceding nuisance, generally §XX or XXI Vehicles)

> *"No commercial vehicle bearing commercial insignias or names shall be parked on any Lot except within an enclosed structure, unless such vehicle is temporarily parked for the purpose of serving such Lot."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §XX/XXI; same in all phase covenants and Amend-Phase1&2.

**Why it matters for filming:** Production trucks, grip trucks, wrapped sprinter vans, and labeled rentals (e.g., "ABC Camera Rentals") would violate this if parked on the lot in view, unless you treat them as "temporarily parked for the purpose of serving such Lot" (the same exception used for plumbers, landscapers, etc.). A short shoot likely qualifies; a multi-day commercial production parked on-lot likely does not.

### 2f. Temporary structures (Section XX)

> *"No structure of a temporary character, tent, shack, garage, barn, or other outbuilding, or trailer, mobile home, house trailer, recreational vehicle, truck camper or similar facility, shall be used on any Lot at any time as a residence or storage facility, either temporarily or permanently. No trailer, tent, boat, recreational vehicle, camper or stripped down, wrecked, junked or inoperable vehicle shall be kept, parked, stored, repaired or maintained on any Lot, street or common areas, unless completely concealed from view."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §XX (matched across all phase covenants).

**Why it matters for filming:** A production base camp — pop-up tents, equipment trailers, talent trailers, honey wagons — is squarely targeted by this language. The clause is keyed to use "as a residence or storage facility," so a brief shoot tent is arguably fine; multi-day storage of gear in a trailer on the lot is not.

### 2g. Signs (Section XXII / XXIII)

> *"No signs of any kind shall be displayed to the public view on any Lot except one professional sign of not more than three (3) square feet advertising the property for sale or rent."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §XXII (matched across all phases). The 2024 Flag & Political Sign Policy carves out flags and political signs per Texas statute.

**Why it matters for filming:** Filming-permit notices, location signs, "Quiet — filming" signs, sponsor signage in shots, etc., would technically violate this without ACC approval.

### 2h. Architectural Control — written approval for any "structure" or "alteration" (Section IV)

> *"No building, fence, wall, or other structure shall be erected, placed, commenced or maintained upon any building or Lot ... nor shall any exterior addition to or change or alteration therein be made to any Lot until the plans and specifications ... shall have been submitted to and approved in writing by [the] Architectural Committee."*
> — *Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas*, §IV (matched across all phases).

**Why it matters for filming:** This applies to *physical structures and exterior alterations*, not to filming activity itself. But large scenic dressing (e.g., a fake façade, scaffolded lighting truss, a temporary deck) that alters the exterior could be challenged.

## 3. Common-area / Marina restrictions (relevant if you film off-lot)

From *Marina and Homeowners' Island Rules* (2011):

- Rule 7: *"Do not use the common areas for any activity that may be deemed a nuisance."*
- Rule 14: *"No overnight camping or use of the area after 10:00 p.m. without prior approval from the LBHOA Board of Directors."*
- **Rule 15: *"To reserve the area for large parties, submit a letter of request to the LBHOA Board of Directors, four (4) weeks prior to the event. State the responsible property owner or resident, date, time, and number of people attending. Numbers will be limited to what is considered safe and not likely to cause a disturbance. **No commercial use will be allowed.**"***

This is the **only express "no commercial use" clause** in the entire document set — and it applies **only to the Marina and Homeowners' Island common areas**, not to private lots. You cannot do commercial filming on the Marina/Island. You also need 4-week advance Board approval to reserve those areas for a large gathering, and gatherings after 10 p.m. need Board approval.

## 4. Source of Board authority over rules (and its limits)

The Bylaws and the 1987 Master Declaration both phrase the Board's rule-making power narrowly:

- **First Amended Bylaws, Article VI / Powers and Duties:** *"adopt and publish rules and regulations governing the use of the **Common Area and facilities**, and the personal conduct of the Members and their guests thereof..."*
- **1987 DCCRs, Article 2 §1(a):** *"The right of the Association to make, publish and enforce reasonable Rules and Regulations for the use of the **Common Area and any facilities situated thereon**..."*

The Board's *express* rule-making authority is over **common areas**, not private lots. New restrictions on what you can do on your own lot have to come through an *amendment* to the Restrictive Covenants (which requires owner vote, not a Board vote). This is reinforced by the Curbing Resolution itself, which cites the Common-Area-rules clause as its authority.

In practice: the Board can pass *interpretive* rules and enforce existing covenants, but it cannot validly adopt a new "no filming" rule without amending the covenants.

## 5. Enforcement mechanism

- **Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas §General Provisions / Enforcement:** *"The Association or any Owner shall have the right to enforce, by any proceeding at law or in equity, all restrictions, conditions, covenants, reservations, liens, assessments and charges now or hereafter imposed by the provisions of this Declaration."* (Identical clauses in every phase.)

This is what gives both the HOA **and any individual neighbor** standing to sue over a covenant violation. So even if the Board chooses not to act on a complaint about filming, a single unhappy neighbor can.

The Marina rules also describe a graduated penalty: three notices of violation in 12 months = privileges revoked for 1 year, with a hearing right.

## 6. Practical risk assessment for filming at your house

| Filming scenario | Direct restriction? | Indirect risk |
|---|---|---|
| Personal family video / photos | None | None — clearly "residential purposes." |
| YouTube-style solo shoot with phone/DSLR, no lighting/sound, no crew | None | None. |
| Influencer / small-team shoot — modest lighting, on-site only, no signage, no extra parking | None | Low. Could be tagged under "residential purposes only" if a complaint arises, but defensible. |
| Friends-and-family event with a videographer | None | Low; event noise/light/parking risk applies to any party. |
| **Paid commercial shoot** (brand content, real-estate photography for hire, music video, short film, location rental to a producer) | None *express* | **Material risk.** The "single-family residential purposes only" + "no commercial vehicles" + "no temporary structures used for storage" + nuisance + sign + lighting + sound clauses all stack against it. ACC written approval is the safe path. |
| Drones over your own lot | None | None directly, but FAA rules and the Security Policy's "no recording neighbors" principle could be invoked if your drone overflies adjacent property. |
| Production trucks / talent trailers / base camp on the lot | None *express* | High — directly implicates the commercial-vehicle and temporary-structure clauses. |
| Outside-of-daylight shoot with lights | None *express* | High — exterior-lighting nuisance clause; complaints likely. |
| Outdoor playback/PA speakers | None *express* | Requires ACC approval per the sound-devices clause. |
| Filming on the Marina or Homeowners' Island | Marina Rule 15 | **Express prohibition on commercial use.** Personal use is fine within Marina rules; large gatherings need 4-week Board approval. |

---

## 7. Bottom-line recommendation

If "filming at my house" means **personal video or photography by family/guests, with no production footprint visible from the street**, *nothing in the covenants restricts you* and no HOA approval is required.

If it means **a commercial production, a paid shoot, a rental of your property as a filming location, a crew with lights/sound/trucks/trailers, signage, or anything past 10 p.m. that is visible or audible to neighbors**, you do not have an express "no filming" rule against you — **but** you have a stack of broadly worded covenants (residential-use-only, nuisance, commercial-vehicles, temporary-structures, lighting, sound, signage) that the Board or any neighbor can credibly invoke. The conservative path is to apply to the **Architectural Control Committee in writing** before any commercial or large-footprint shoot, citing §I's "unless otherwise approved by the Architectural Control Committee" carve-out, and to keep equipment/vehicles concealed and noise/light off your neighbors.
