LAS BRISAS HOA - FILMING RESTRICTIONS REVIEW
=============================================

Source: https://www.ciaservices.com/cia-community-documents.php?id=194432
Scope reviewed: 41 governing documents (Articles, Bylaws + Amendment,
Master Declaration, all 10 Phase Restrictive Covenants + amendments, all
Annexation Declarations, ACC Extensions, Management Certificate, Policies
(Collection, Records Production, Records Retention, Security, Flag/Sign),
Marina & Curbing Rules).


BOTTOM LINE
-----------
There is NO explicit prohibition on filming, photography, video recording,
drones, or media production anywhere in the Las Brisas governing documents.
None of the words "film, video, photograph, drone, broadcast, cinema,
motion picture, production" appear in any covenant, bylaw, or rule as a
restricted activity. The only direct hits for "video / photograph / camera"
are administrative (records-copy fee schedule) or about home security
cameras.

That said, the HOA does have several INDIRECT restrictions that an
enforcement-minded Board or a complaining neighbor could try to use to
challenge filming at your house - especially commercial filming, large
crews, equipment trucks, or anything that creates noise/light/traffic.


1. DIRECT KEYWORD SCAN - WHAT DOES NOT EXIST
--------------------------------------------
Across all 41 documents, 0 hits for: film, drone, UAV, broadcast, cinema,
motion picture, media production, crew, lens.

Hits for video / photo / camera, in context:

  - "video / photograph" -> Policy-Records-Production: per-page copy fees
    for DVDs, VHS, photographs. Administrative, NOT a restriction.

  - "camera, recording" -> Policy-Security (Mar 2024): permits security
    cameras on your own property; restricts them from facing/recording
    neighbors. NOT a restriction on personal filming; only on surveillance
    pointed at others.

    Verbatim, Security Policy Section 2: "Security cameras may be installed
    on an owner's property. Security cameras may face into and record any
    portion of the owner's property. For privacy reasons, security cameras
    shall not face into or record the private property of others such as a
    neighboring property."

    That language is about fixed surveillance cameras, but a careful reader
    should note the "private property of others" principle - the HOA has
    on the record that it considers pointing recording equipment at
    neighbors a privacy violation. A hostile neighbor could try to extend
    this to a film camera pointed across a property line.


2. INDIRECT RESTRICTIONS THAT COULD BE INVOKED
----------------------------------------------
These appear in essentially identical form across the master Restrictive
Covenants for Las Brisas, the Declaration of Covenants, Conditions &
Restrictions (1987), the Amendment to Phase I & II Restrictive Covenants
(1989), and every per-phase Restrictive Covenants document (Phases I-X,
including all Phase VI amendments).

2a. SINGLE-FAMILY RESIDENTIAL USE ONLY (Section I - Land Use)

    "Said Lots be known and described as single-family residential Lots.
    Unless otherwise approved by the Architectural Control Committee,
    such Lots are to be used for single-family residential purposes only."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section I (and identical or
    near-identical text in Amend-Phase1&2 Section I, RC-Phase1 through
    RC-Phase10 Section I, including all Phase VI Amended & Restated
    versions).

    Why it matters for filming: "Residential purposes only" is the standard
    hook HOAs use to argue that COMMERCIAL filming (paid shoots,
    productions, rentals to film companies) is a prohibited "non-residential
    use." Personal/family filming is almost certainly not implicated. Note
    the carve-out - the ACC CAN approve other uses. That means commercial
    filming explicitly REQUIRES written ACC approval under this clause.

2b. NUISANCE / NOXIOUS / OFFENSIVE ACTIVITY (Section XXI or XXII)

    "No noxious or offensive activity shall be carried on upon any Lot or
    common area, nor shall anything be done thereon which may be or may
    become an annoyance or nuisance to the neighborhood."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section XXI (matched in every
    phase covenant and the Phase 1&2 amendment).

    Why it matters for filming: This is the catch-all. A neighbor who
    finds your shoot disruptive - closed road, generator noise, crew foot
    traffic, late hours - can complain that it is an "annoyance or
    nuisance to the neighborhood." It is intentionally vague.

2c. EXTERIOR LIGHTING (same section)

    "No exterior lighting of any sort shall be installed or maintained on
    a Lot where the light source is offensive or a nuisance to neighboring
    property (except reasonable landscape lighting that has the approval
    of the Architectural Committee)."
    -- Same section as above, all documents listed in 2b.

    Why it matters for filming: Movie lights, HMIs, balloon lights, and
    even consumer LED panels can be argued to be "offensive or a nuisance"
    to neighbors. Approval is required even for landscape lighting; a
    literal reading would require approval for any production lighting.

2d. EXTERIOR SPEAKERS / SOUND DEVICES (same section)

    "No exterior speakers, horns, whistles, bells or other sound devices
    (except security devices used exclusively to protect the lot and the
    improvements located thereon) shall be placed or used upon any Lot
    without approval of the Architectural Committee. Telephones will be
    allowed outdoors."
    -- Same section, all phase covenants.

    Why it matters for filming: Outdoor PA, playback speakers, bullhorns,
    or boom-mic monitoring through a powered speaker would arguably need
    ACC approval. Lavalier mics and a single boom would not.

2e. COMMERCIAL VEHICLES (Section preceding nuisance)

    "No commercial vehicle bearing commercial insignias or names shall be
    parked on any Lot except within an enclosed structure, unless such
    vehicle is temporarily parked for the purpose of serving such Lot."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section XX/XXI; same in all
    phase covenants and Amend-Phase1&2.

    Why it matters for filming: Production trucks, grip trucks, wrapped
    sprinter vans, and labeled rentals (e.g., "ABC Camera Rentals") would
    violate this if parked on the lot in view, unless you treat them as
    "temporarily parked for the purpose of serving such Lot" (the same
    exception used for plumbers, landscapers, etc.). A short shoot likely
    qualifies; a multi-day commercial production parked on-lot likely
    does not.

2f. TEMPORARY STRUCTURES (Section XX)

    "No structure of a temporary character, tent, shack, garage, barn, or
    other outbuilding, or trailer, mobile home, house trailer, recreational
    vehicle, truck camper or similar facility, shall be used on any Lot at
    any time as a residence or storage facility, either temporarily or
    permanently. No trailer, tent, boat, recreational vehicle, camper or
    stripped down, wrecked, junked or inoperable vehicle shall be kept,
    parked, stored, repaired or maintained on any Lot, street or common
    areas, unless completely concealed from view."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section XX (matched across
    all phase covenants).

    Why it matters for filming: A production base camp - pop-up tents,
    equipment trailers, talent trailers, honey wagons - is squarely
    targeted by this language. The clause is keyed to use "as a residence
    or storage facility," so a brief shoot tent is arguably fine;
    multi-day storage of gear in a trailer on the lot is not.

2g. SIGNS (Section XXII / XXIII)

    "No signs of any kind shall be displayed to the public view on any
    Lot except one professional sign of not more than three (3) square
    feet advertising the property for sale or rent."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section XXII (matched across
    all phases). The 2024 Flag & Political Sign Policy carves out flags
    and political signs per Texas statute.

    Why it matters for filming: Filming-permit notices, location signs,
    "Quiet - filming" signs, sponsor signage in shots, etc., would
    technically violate this without ACC approval.

2h. ARCHITECTURAL CONTROL - WRITTEN APPROVAL FOR ANY "STRUCTURE" OR
    "ALTERATION" (Section IV)

    "No building, fence, wall, or other structure shall be erected,
    placed, commenced or maintained upon any building or Lot ... nor
    shall any exterior addition to or change or alteration therein be
    made to any Lot until the plans and specifications ... shall have
    been submitted to and approved in writing by [the] Architectural
    Committee."
    -- Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, Section IV (matched across
    all phases).

    Why it matters for filming: This applies to PHYSICAL structures and
    exterior alterations, not to filming activity itself. But large
    scenic dressing (e.g., a fake facade, scaffolded lighting truss, a
    temporary deck) that alters the exterior could be challenged.


3. COMMON-AREA / MARINA RESTRICTIONS (relevant if you film off-lot)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
From Marina and Homeowners' Island Rules (2011):

  - Rule 7: "Do not use the common areas for any activity that may be
    deemed a nuisance."

  - Rule 14: "No overnight camping or use of the area after 10:00 p.m.
    without prior approval from the LBHOA Board of Directors."

  - Rule 15: "To reserve the area for large parties, submit a letter of
    request to the LBHOA Board of Directors, four (4) weeks prior to the
    event. State the responsible property owner or resident, date, time,
    and number of people attending. Numbers will be limited to what is
    considered safe and not likely to cause a disturbance. NO COMMERCIAL
    USE WILL BE ALLOWED."

This is the ONLY EXPRESS "no commercial use" clause in the entire document
set - and it applies ONLY to the Marina and Homeowners' Island common
areas, not to private lots. You cannot do commercial filming on the
Marina/Island. You also need 4-week advance Board approval to reserve
those areas for a large gathering, and gatherings after 10 p.m. need
Board approval.


4. SOURCE OF BOARD AUTHORITY OVER RULES (AND ITS LIMITS)
--------------------------------------------------------
The Bylaws and the 1987 Master Declaration both phrase the Board's
rule-making power narrowly:

  - First Amended Bylaws, Article VI / Powers and Duties: "adopt and
    publish rules and regulations governing the use of the COMMON AREA
    and facilities, and the personal conduct of the Members and their
    guests thereof..."

  - 1987 DCCRs, Article 2 Section 1(a): "The right of the Association to
    make, publish and enforce reasonable Rules and Regulations for the
    use of the COMMON AREA and any facilities situated thereon..."

The Board's express rule-making authority is over COMMON AREAS, not
private lots. New restrictions on what you can do on your own lot have
to come through an AMENDMENT to the Restrictive Covenants (which
requires owner vote, not a Board vote). This is reinforced by the
Curbing Resolution itself, which cites the Common-Area-rules clause as
its authority.

In practice: the Board can pass interpretive rules and enforce existing
covenants, but it cannot validly adopt a new "no filming" rule without
amending the covenants.


5. ENFORCEMENT MECHANISM
------------------------
Restrictive Covenants for Las Brisas, General Provisions / Enforcement:
"The Association or any Owner shall have the right to enforce, by any
proceeding at law or in equity, all restrictions, conditions, covenants,
reservations, liens, assessments and charges now or hereafter imposed by
the provisions of this Declaration." (Identical clauses in every phase.)

This is what gives both the HOA AND any individual neighbor standing to
sue over a covenant violation. So even if the Board chooses not to act
on a complaint about filming, a single unhappy neighbor can.

The Marina rules also describe a graduated penalty: three notices of
violation in 12 months = privileges revoked for 1 year, with a hearing
right.


6. PRACTICAL RISK ASSESSMENT FOR FILMING AT YOUR HOUSE
------------------------------------------------------

  Personal family video / photos
    - Direct restriction: None
    - Indirect risk: None - clearly "residential purposes."

  YouTube-style solo shoot with phone/DSLR, no lighting/sound, no crew
    - Direct restriction: None
    - Indirect risk: None.

  Influencer / small-team shoot - modest lighting, on-site only, no
  signage, no extra parking
    - Direct restriction: None
    - Indirect risk: Low. Could be tagged under "residential purposes
      only" if a complaint arises, but defensible.

  Friends-and-family event with a videographer
    - Direct restriction: None
    - Indirect risk: Low; event noise/light/parking risk applies to any
      party.

  PAID COMMERCIAL SHOOT (brand content, real-estate photography for hire,
  music video, short film, location rental to a producer)
    - Direct restriction: None EXPRESS
    - Indirect risk: MATERIAL. The "single-family residential purposes
      only" + "no commercial vehicles" + "no temporary structures used
      for storage" + nuisance + sign + lighting + sound clauses all
      stack against it. ACC written approval is the safe path.

  Drones over your own lot
    - Direct restriction: None
    - Indirect risk: None directly, but FAA rules and the Security
      Policy's "no recording neighbors" principle could be invoked if
      your drone overflies adjacent property.

  Production trucks / talent trailers / base camp on the lot
    - Direct restriction: None EXPRESS
    - Indirect risk: HIGH - directly implicates the commercial-vehicle
      and temporary-structure clauses.

  Outside-of-daylight shoot with lights
    - Direct restriction: None EXPRESS
    - Indirect risk: HIGH - exterior-lighting nuisance clause;
      complaints likely.

  Outdoor playback/PA speakers
    - Direct restriction: None EXPRESS
    - Indirect risk: Requires ACC approval per the sound-devices clause.

  Filming on the Marina or Homeowners' Island
    - Direct restriction: Marina Rule 15
    - Indirect risk: EXPRESS PROHIBITION ON COMMERCIAL USE. Personal use
      is fine within Marina rules; large gatherings need 4-week Board
      approval.


7. BOTTOM-LINE RECOMMENDATION
-----------------------------
If "filming at my house" means PERSONAL video or photography by family
or guests, with no production footprint visible from the street, NOTHING
in the covenants restricts you and NO HOA APPROVAL IS REQUIRED.

If it means a COMMERCIAL production, a paid shoot, a rental of your
property as a filming location, a crew with lights/sound/trucks/trailers,
signage, or anything past 10 p.m. that is visible or audible to
neighbors, you do not have an express "no filming" rule against you -
BUT you have a stack of broadly worded covenants (residential-use-only,
nuisance, commercial-vehicles, temporary-structures, lighting, sound,
signage) that the Board or any neighbor can credibly invoke.

The conservative path is to apply to the ARCHITECTURAL CONTROL COMMITTEE
in writing before any commercial or large-footprint shoot, citing
Section I's "unless otherwise approved by the Architectural Control
Committee" carve-out, and to keep equipment/vehicles concealed and
noise/light off your neighbors.
